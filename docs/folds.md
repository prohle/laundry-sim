# How garments are folded

Standard retail and laundry practice. These sequences are established technique, not a fetched
citation — the search returned nothing usable, so this is written from knowledge and should be checked
against a folding guide before it is presented as sourced.

## The three principles

1. **Lay flat and smooth first.** Every method begins with the garment flat and smoothed. A fold made
   on a wrinkled garment stays wrinkled.
2. **Sleeves fold back on themselves.** A sleeve is folded along its own line, never dragged across the
   body, so the shoulder keeps its shape.
3. **The final fold is made with a flat edge.** A board or a blade sweeps the fabric over. Lifting two
   corners and letting gravity flop the middle is not a fold; it is a carry.

## The sequences

### T-shirt — retail / board fold (four folds)

1. Face down, smooth.
2. Left third (sleeve and side panel) to the centre; the sleeve folds back on itself.
3. Right third to the centre.
4. Bottom third up.
5. Top third down. The collar sits on top.

Result: a rectangle roughly one third the width and one third the height.

### T-shirt — KonMari (three folds)

1. Sleeves in to the centre.
2. Fold in half lengthwise.
3. Fold in thirds or quarters so it stands upright in a drawer.

### Pants (three folds)

1. Flat, front up, legs together, crotch smoothed.
2. One leg over the other, lengthwise.
3. Waist to hem, in half.
4. In thirds for stacking.

### Shorts (two folds)

Same as pants, one fold fewer: legs together, then in half.

### Button shirt / jacket (four folds)

1. Buttoned, face down.
2. Each sleeve folded across the back, no crease at the shoulder.
3. Half lengthwise.
4. In thirds.

### Dress (two to three folds)

1. Back up, smooth.
2. Sides to the centre (or half lengthwise).
3. In half or thirds, skirt tucked inside.

## Mapping to a four-flap board

A retail folding board is a flat panel with four flaps. Every sequence above is expressible as a flap
order if the garment is oriented on the board correctly:

| garment | orientation | flap order |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirt | body across the width, hem to the front | left, right, bottom, top |
| pants | legs across the width, waist to the back | left, right, bottom, top |
| shorts | as pants | left, right, bottom |
| jacket | body across the width | left, right, bottom, top |
| dress | hem to the front | left, right, bottom |

The board folds **across** the garment; the orientation of the garment decides which axis each flap
folds.

## What this means for the machine

The Laundroid used two arms. Arms need to reach, grip, and carry; the reach problem is what blocked
every sleeve fold in this project. A board needs none of that: four hinges, one smoothing bar, and the
garment lies still while the board does the work.

The arms stay as the **loader** — they place the garment on the board — and are retired from folding.
That keeps the cabinet and removes the reach limit.
